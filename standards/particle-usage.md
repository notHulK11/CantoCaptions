# Particle usage

Which particle a speaker chose changes what the line means, so it must be written as said.
This page explains the particles that are most often confused, with example sentences and
the near-miss alternatives, then the question particles, some compounds, and set phrases
that usually take one particle. The character for each syllable and tone is on the
[particles](particles.md) page.

## Single Particles

### 喇 laa3

喇 laa3 is generally used for conveying information or change of state. It has a more neutral or serious tone.

#### Usage 1: Relevance, change of state

* 我返嚟喇 - "I'm back!" The speaker is informing listeners that they have come back, a change of state that
  is relevant to the current situation. 我返嚟啦 (laa1) is extremely uncommon, as it seems to be expressing
  a proposal from the speaker that they will come back, rather than expressing that they have come back.
* 我哋食飯喇 - "Time to eat." The speaker is stating that the time has come to eat in a matter-of-fact way,
  not making a proposal for the listener to respond to. If they had said "我哋食飯啦" (laa1), that would be
  a proposal, more commonly used with 一齊 to propose to eat together.

#### Usage 2: Warnings

* 你都係快啲走喇 - "You really should go". The speaker is warning the listener that it would be better if they
  went. 你都係快啲走啦 (laa1) is very possible as well, but serves more as a reminder of a previously known fact,
  not as new information.
* 你唔好再講喇 - "You'd better not say any more!" The speaker here is expressing a serious and necessary command,
  rather than a proposition. 你唔好再講啦 (laa1) is possible also, but expresses that the listener should already
  know not to say more, making it less of a warning and more of a reminder or proposal.

#### Usage 3: Expletives, Outbursts

* 弊喇，死喇 bai3 laa3, sei2 laa3 - These are both non-targeted outbursts that express the speaker's assessment of
  the situation, kind of like "we're screwed". 死啦 sei2 laa1 is also very common, but it's used as an insulting
  request "Just die" (or something like that). In very extreme circumstances, 死喇！ (laa3) may also be used as a
  targeted warning, like a declaration of the speaker's enemies' death (你同我死喇！). So while 死啦 is often an insult
  or a tongue-in-cheek proposition, laa3 can, in real circumstances which may involve violence, be used as a threat.

#### Usage 4: Matter-of-Fact assessment of a situation, expressing a decision

* 今日天氣真係太熱喇. Here the user is expressing that the weather is "simply too hot", stating it as a fact rather than a feeling.
  天氣太熱啊 (aa3) is also very common, and expresses the speaker's subjective opinion rather than implying any factual or
  definitive relevance to other speakers. "太過份喇！" is also another very common such usage.
* 一定係佢喇 - "It's certainly him". The speaker is sure of a fact, and stating this as a relevant fact. 一定係佢啦 (laa1) is also
  possible, but instead seeks to express that the listener should already know that it was him ("I mean, it's obviously him").
  So while 喇 laa3 here conveys a certain fact, 啦 laa1 expresses obviousness or a presumption.

### Traditional goodbyes

* 我走先喇 "I'm heading out". 我走先啊 is usually a typo or mishearing for this.
* 再見喇 "See you later". While 再見啊 is probably a typo for this, 再見啦 (laa1) is actually sometimes possible, though it is less
  common. 再見啦 sounds more like a positive statement that the speaker really expects to see the listener later ("I'll see you later, ok?").
  喇 laa3 is the more straightforward and common version, though.

### 啦 laa1

啦 laa1 is generally used for seeking approval or common ground from a listener. It can be used playfully or in a joking
way, but that doesn't necessarily mean it isn't mean-spirited.

#### Usage 1: Request, proposition

* 你聽日先去啦 - "How about you go tomorrow?" Here the laa1 is conveyed in "how about..." as a more polite way of seeking the
  listener's approval for the request. Saying 你聽日先去喇 (laa3) might be possible in some circumstances, but it sounds very
  rude, like a flat "Go tomorrow."

#### Usage 2: Invitation, acceptance

* 好啦 hou2 laa1 "Alright". The speaker is showing they have accepted a proposal, or that they have
  been considering a decision internally and just now decided to move ahead with it. This is different from 好喇 (laa3), which
  is more like an "Alright, now", which often implies it's time to do something. These two are very difficult to separate
  because of their similarity in meaning.
* 噉我坐嗰度啦 "Ok, I'll sit there then." The speaker may be talking to themselves, in which case they are showing that they were
  considering where to sit and have now decided to sit there, or they may be saying this to another person, in which case they
  could be communicating their decision politely and also trying to get approval from the listener to sit there.
  我坐嗰度喇 (laa3) would be more like "I'm going to sit there", a matter-of-fact statement.

#### Usage 3: Reluctance, sarcasm

* 好啦… "Well, alright..."
* 生活過得去啦 "Life is okay, I guess". The speaker is expressing disdain or discomfort with their statement, in an almost satirical
  way. Here they really seem to mean that life is not all that OK. 生活過得OK囖 (lo1) can be used the same way, though this sounds
  even more flippant and uncaring, as if the speaker wants to pretend they don't care about whether life is good or bad.
* 你太大隻啦 "You're just too buff". This could be the speaker being sarcastic, saying the listener is buff as a joke rather than
  really meaning it, or perhaps as a playful compliment without much meaning behind it. If they really are saying it as a genuine-sounding
  compliment, it would probably have emphasis builders like 真係, 一定 or neutral expression particles like 啊 aa3 or 喇 laa3.

#### Usage 4: Listing things

* 你要學嘅就係步法啦，姿勢啦… Here the speaker is using laa1 to straightforwardly list or enumerate items.

### 嗱 laa4

嗱 naa4/laa4 can be used as a "here you go", and sometimes this gets merged into its particle usage as a compound particle (e.g. 啊嗱 aa3naa4,
吖嗱 aa1naa4). However, the main use of 嗱 as a final particle is as a questioning or surprised version of 喇 laa3. See the Question Particle
section for more details.

### 啊 aa3

啊 aa3 is an all-use particle that can be applied to almost any sentence. In general, it expresses that speakers feelings or opinions, rather
than providing hard facts or adding other complex nuances to the statement.

#### Usage 1: Simple expression

* 噉樣唔得啊 "There's no way!". The speaker is expressing a feeling that something can't be. They may be upset or adamant about it, but either way
  they are simply expressing to listeners their general viewpoint.

#### Usage 2: Parsing statements

* 噉啊，雖然係佢做啊，但係你都要揾辦法 - Here aa3 is mainly used to parse out the statements and maintain the flow of speech, rather than adding additional
  meaning. Similar to "uh" or "well" in English.

#### Usage 3: General exclamations

* 唔好啊！ "No!" or "Don't!". 冇啊 could potentially be a typo.
* 快啲啊！ "Faster!" or "Quick!". While 快啲啦 and 快啲喇 are both more common, 快啲啊 is less of a direct command and more of a speaker simply expressing their
  desire for the listener to go faster. Usually this way of speaking indicates the speaker is under distress or doesn't have time to collect
  their thoughts. Thus, they are foregoing a more nuanced command and simply expressing their need for things to move faster. Similarly with "走啊！".

### 吖 aa1

#### Usage 1: Polite or subservient request

* 求下你吖，幫我吖 "Please, I'm begging you, help me" The speaker is acting subservient to the listener by using 吖 here. 求下你啦 is less common but also used,
  with a less subservient and more assertive tone.
* 坐吖 "Please sit" Asking someone to sit the way a waiter would ask a customer to sit down, very polite. 坐啦 sounds a bit impolite by comparison.
* 不如我哋去吖 "How about we go?" a softer request than 不如我哋去啦, which is more like "Let's go".

#### Usage 2: Indicating an option or possibility

While 啦 lists things in general, 吖 can be used more specifically to list options, things to be selected. Similarly, this usage also matches
呢 very closely, where the speaker can use 吖 to ask a hypothetical question.

* 你鍾意綠色吖，定係藍色啊？ - "Do you like green, or do you like blue?" The 吖 here is just used to give a little time to draw attention to the green
  option before proceeding to the next and final option.

#### Usage 3: Rhetorical question

* 咁嘈，我點可以瞓覺吖？ - The speaker is asking a rhetorical question, emphasising the impossibility of sleeping. However, they are not trying to
  ask confrontationally, simply trying to convey the obvious and unavoidable truth. If they wanted to be more confrontational, they might use
  唧 zek1 in this question instead. If they want to ask in a more neutral way, to get the listener to really consider the question, they would
  probably use 呢 le1/ne1.

### 呀 aa4

呀 is mainly used as a question particle. See the Question Particle section for details.

However, there are some other notable uses of 呀 aa4:

#### Usage 1: To get someone's attention when addressing them

* 阿紅呀，到你喇. Here the speaker wants to get 阿紅's attention to give them relevant information (it's their turn). 啊 aa3 is also used when
  addressing someone, but it's generally used to simply call someone's name, rather than trying to call their attention. For example,
  阿紅啊，到你喇 sounds more like the speaker is pouting or unhappy with 阿紅, which is more common of children talking to each other in this
  situation.

#### Usage 2: Conditional statements

* Rarely, 呀 will be used after the first clause of a conditional statement, similarly to "嘅話". 如果你唔認呀，我真係幫唔到你 ("If you don't realize it,
 there's no way I can help you). 啊 aa3 may be a typo or mishearing in these scenarios.

### 囖 lo1

#### Usage 1: Stating an obvious option, usually that went unnoticed

* A: 你會點call佢啊？  B: 電話囖 - speaker is flippantly making a statement that they will use a phone, emphasizing the obviousness
  of the choice, and assuming that the listener did not take it into account.

#### Usage 2: Just do... 咪得囖

* 佢對你唔好，你同佢分手咪得囖 - "He's so bad to you, just break up with him". 咪…囖 is a common construction with this meaning, though 啦 can be used
  as a rarer, softer option.

#### Usage 3: Strong opinion

* 我唔同意囖！ "I don't agree at all!"

### 咯 lo3

#### Usage 1: A cordial request

* 去玩遊戲咯 - "Let us go play", used to make a request in a polite and upstanding way.

#### Usage 2: 喇 sound-alike

Sometimes 喇 laa3 is converted by speakers to 咯 lo3 as a sound-alike with the same meaning and uses. This is rare but does happen when speakers
are trying to be funny or use a different style. Most often seen with 係咯 (agreeing, similarly to 係喇)

### 啫 ze1

#### Usage 1: Diminutive / "Only"

This usage is similar to 咋 zaa3

* 我講笑啫 - "I was only joking"

### 唧 zek1

Very often confused with 啫 ze1, and the two are not always easy to tell apart. However, 唧 zek1 is generally the right option if in a question.

#### Usage 1: Pointed question

* 點會唔明唧？ - "How would they not understand?"

#### Usage 2: Gleeful, excited, or anxious feeling

* 好彩唧！ - "So lucky!" (often used like "that was close!" in English)

## Question-Specific Particles

### Tone 4 呀 aa4 / 嘎 gaa4 / 嗱 laa4 etc.

Tone 4 particles are almost universally used to form a question in the absence of a question word. In these cases,
they simply replace the tone 3 neutral question version of the statement to create the question version. For example:

* 係佢做㗎 "It was him" -> 係佢做嘎？  "It was him?"
* 你行咗好遠啊 "You walked really far" -> 你行咗好遠呀？ "You walked really far?"
* 今次我合格喇 "This time I passed" -> 今次我合格嗱？ "This time I passed?"
* 佢講笑咋 "He was just joking" -> 佢講笑喳？ "He was just joking?"
* 佢好犀利喎 "He's so impressive" -> 佢好犀利啝？ "He's (actually) really impressive?"

啝 wo4 is unique in that it often is not used as a question, due to it sounding a bit unnatural. However, it can be used
the same way without the question mark - it just gives an extra degree of shock in that case. The other particles may
very rarely be used as shock-modifiers without the question mark, but that is exceptionally rare, so it's more likely just
a typo if the question mark was forgotten.

### 咩 me1

Can be paired with either a positive or negative statement to make a question, usually rhetorical or astonished.

* 你真係食得落㗎咩？ - "You can really eat that?"
* 噉樣唔係叫跳舞咩？ - "You don't call this dancing?"

## Compound Particles

### gaa laa

- Primary form: 㗎喇 gaa3 laa3
- Basic usage: Used to assert a fact that the speaker feels the listener ought to know. The 㗎 may be used either to express
  that the fact is opposed or contradicts previous knowledge, or it may simply be a carryover from the nominalization usage
  of 嘅, which is used for describing states or properties of things.
- Alternatives: 㗎啦 (sarcasm, obviousness), 㗎嗱 gaa3 laa4 / 嘎嗱 gaa4 laa4 (question)

### aa6 maa5

- Primary form: 𠻺嗎 aa6 maa5
- Basic usage: Expresses disbelief or incredulity.
- Alternatives: many particles have a rare tone 6 version which is used the same way and carries over the tone 3 particle
  meaning. For example, 咤嗎 zaa6 maa5 combines the "just" meaning from 咋 zaa3 with the disbelief from 𠻺嗎.

### aa1 maa3

- Primary form: 吖嘛 aa1 maa3
- Basic usage: Used to appeal to the listener for common understanding, similar to "right?" or "you know?". Less assertive and
  more diminutive than using a straightforward 啦 laa1 in the same way.
- Alternatives: 啦嘛 carries over meaning from laa3, 𠺢嘛 ga1 maa3 carries over the meaning from gaa3, and 吒嘛 za1 maa3 carries
  over meaning from zaa3

### 下哇 ha6 waa5

Generally a contracted version of 係咪啊.

## Special Phrases

### 我都話咗…

When a speaker wants to say "I told you so", or express that they have already said something, there are a few different
particles that can be used.

* 我都話咗…嚛 (lok3) - More negative connotation. Implies that the listener ought to have known better, blaming the listener
  of oversight or fault.
* 我都話咗…㗎啦 (gaa3 laa1) - Not as stern as 嚛, but still expresses that the listener should have already known this information.
  More of a playful "I told you so" rather than an accusation. The 㗎 is not strictly necessary, but sounds more natural as it
  is often used in situations where the speaker is trying to soften saying something in opposition to the listener.
* 我都話咗…㗎喇 (gaa3 laa3) - Used only rarely, a very flat and neutral way to state that something was said. Does not imply the
  listener knew what was said at the time. It would make more sense to say this without "都", the word that intensifies the action,
  話 to speak, because the speaker doesn't appear to be trying to make an accusation or citing common knowledge.

### 可能…

When saying that something may happen or is likely to have happened, there are a few different particles that are often used.

* 可能係噉啦 (laa1) "It's probably like that". Most common, generally used on more optimistic hypotheses. Seeks the listener's
  approval or expresses common understanding or obviousness.
* 可能係噉呢 (le1) Very similar in usage to laa1, mainly differentiated by sound. More neutral, like the speaker is considering
  the options as they speak ("It could be...").
* 可能係噉喇 (laa3) used with more negative or serious implications.

### Set phrases that generally use 喇 laa3

- 太好喇 "too awesome!"
- 就得喇 "... that's all/that's it"
- 就好喇 "... that would be great"

In general, use of 而家, 已經, or the change-of-state verb aspect 咗 are strongly collocated with 喇 laa3.
